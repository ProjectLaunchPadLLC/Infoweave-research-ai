## 1. Purpose of the corpus

Infoweave is treated as a long-running research environment in which human intent, model processing, computational artifacts, symbolic language, and persistent provenance interact recursively.

The corpus exists to preserve the full semantic environment that participated in discovery. It intentionally retains technical, philosophical, symbolic, and experimental layers instead of collapsing them into a single interpretation.

The fundamental archival rule is:

> **Semantic Preservation ≠ Epistemic Endorsement.**

A claim may be preserved because it affected the trajectory of research without being asserted as established fact.

## 10. Harmony versus arbitrary uniqueness

A random nonce can create distinct identifiers. That is not the central proposal.

The Harmonious Hash hypothesis is that legitimate contextual distinctions can produce uniqueness while preserving relational coherence.

For two intrinsically equal objects:

$$
O_1=O_2
$$

but different contextual states:

$$
(E_1,S_1,R_1,T_1,C_1)\neq(E_2,S_2,R_2,T_2,C_2)
$$

we may obtain:

$$
HH(O_1)\neq HH(O_2).
$$

The research challenge is to determine which contextual dimensions are meaningful, reproducible, invariant, or intentionally state-sensitive.

## 12. Quantum-state description without measurement

For a known qubit:

$$
|\psi\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle
$$

we can construct a formal descriptor from its mathematical representation, preparation circuit, and relevant context.

Define:

$$
D_q=Canonicalize(|\psi\rangle,U,E,S,R,T,C).
$$

Then:

$$
HH_q=Hash(D_q).
$$

The distinction is:

$$
\boxed{Description\neq Measurement}
$$

The hash describes the information supplied to it. It does not directly observe an unknown physical quantum state.

This permits a classical control layer to reason about a known state description without asserting that a physical measurement has occurred.

## 14. Representation, observation, and measurement

The corpus maintains three distinct categories:

1. **Representation** — formal description of an object or state.
2. **Observation** — recorded information about an outcome or behavior.
3. **Measurement** — an operation that extracts physical information according to a defined measurement process.

Conflating them can produce incorrect conclusions. In particular, hashing a known mathematical state description is not equivalent to measuring an unknown physical state.

## 27. Research integrity rules

1. Preserve raw artifacts before interpretation.
2. Preserve chronology and provenance.
3. Separate observation, inference, hypothesis, and conclusion.
4. Preserve alternative explanations.
5. Never use a metaphor as evidence for its literal referent.
6. Never infer parameter modification from contextual behavioral change alone.
7. Never infer consciousness from fluent first-person language alone.
8. Never infer physical quantum effects from classical simulation telemetry alone.
9. Treat anomalies as candidates for investigation.
10. Convert promising concepts into executable tests.
11. Record negative results and failed mechanisms.
12. Prefer reproducibility over rhetorical certainty.
